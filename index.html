The land

Two hundred acres
and no fence line
in the way

The ranch sits next to Golds Park in the Dale mining district, off Highway 62 on a well-maintained dirt road. Past the office trailer and the yard stacked with iron, the road opens into flat, secluded desert with a mountain range standing along the back of it. There are no marked pitches. You drive in, look around, and camp wherever you like the best.

People come here for the quiet and the room. One guest came back for a second night and couldn't find the spot they'd used the first time. Another had roughly 140 acres entirely to themselves. There's an old iron mine on the property with the pit still open, more off-road routes than you'll get through in one trip, and BLM land right alongside.

This is nomadic camping — self-contained is the word. Nothing is plumbed and nothing is wired. Bring your own water, bring more sunscreen than you think you need, and the desert will handle the rest.

Out here

Something for everyone,
mostly involving dirt

01

Off-road routes

All off-road vehicles are welcome — dirt bikes, side-by-sides, whatever you've trailered in. There are more trails here and on the neighbouring BLM land than you'll finish in one trip.

02

Hiking & climbing

Plenty of terrain straight from camp, with rock worth climbing and a mountain range at the back of the property to walk toward.

03

The old mine

This was a substantial iron mine once, and the pit is still open. It's genuinely interesting to poke around — and a real reason to watch your footing.

04

Gold panning & golf

Teala has met guests with panning gear and a set of golf clubs and balls. Hitting a ball into 200 acres of desert is its own kind of driving range.

05

Target shooting

Shooting is permitted on the land, which is part of why people choose it. Know your backstop and who else is camped out there.

06

Joshua Tree

The North Entrance Station is within a thirty-minute drive — a genuine alternative when the park campgrounds are full or you'd rather have privacy.

Getting in

The precise address comes through after booking, to protect the host's privacy. This is the shape of the arrival.

  1. Off Highway 62

    The turn is off the highway onto a well-maintained dirt road next to Golds Park, in the Dale mining district. Two-wheel drive handles it all the way to the camping area.

  2. The office trailer and the iron yard

    You'll pass an office trailer at the entrance by the highway and a yard with big piles of iron. That's the right place — keep going.

  3. Follow the good road and the signage

    Signs mark the way in. For Daisy May, turn at the stacked rocks on your left and carry on back toward the mountain.

  4. Camp wherever you like the best

    No numbered pitches, no assigned spots. Drive the area, find level ground and a view you want to wake up to, and set up.

  5. Order extras in advance

    Firewood and tent rental are arranged directly with Teala. Message her with roughly when you'll arrive and what you want, so it's waiting.
